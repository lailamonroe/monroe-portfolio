Design for Advertising Final Project

“Wild Takes” — A National Parks Conservation Association Campaign

Wild Takes is a speculative advertising campaign for the National Parks Conservation Association (NPCA) that calls out federal funding cuts and policy rollbacks impacting U.S. national parks. It targets Gen Z and Millennials by treating activism as a conversation, not a lecture.

Role: Solo Campaign Timeline: 2 weeks Tools: Figma / Adobe

Problem

National parks are losing funding, staff, and protection due to federal budget cuts and policy changes. When rangers disappear, maintenance is delayed, wildlife is disrupted, and public safety is compromised. The damage happens quietly, but the impact lasts for decades.

Audience

Gen Z and Millennials care about climate and culture, but traditional activism can feel repetitive and easy to ignore. They respond to honesty, humor, and content that feels shareable and real — not guilt-driven messaging.

Insight

People are desensitized to “save the planet” messaging. If activism wants attention today, it has to speak in the language of culture: memes, sarcasm, and blunt truth — while still delivering real stakes.

Solution

This project flips the script by using humor, irony, and wild animals as “speakers” to make conservation feel current, shareable, and impossible to ignore.

It’s advertising as activism — making federal decisions feel personal and sparking long-term engagement.

Process

Researched how federal cuts impact staffing and visitor safety, then identified why younger audiences tune out traditional nonprofit messaging. Built the campaign around a single creative system: “wild takes” from animals that feel like meme captions, but point to real consequences (lost rangers, unsafe parks, weakened protections).

Developed deliverables as if it were a real rollout: print ads + web placements + social concepts — with consistent tone, typography, and a bold, conversational voice.
